Build a DCP

In the app

On Project, drop the master on Assets, or press + Video and drop it on the Picture track. If the mix is its own file, press + Audio and drop it on Sound. In Properties, set Title, and under Output set Folder. Press Build DCP. The package is a new folder inside that one, named after the title. Sound comes from the video unless you added a WAV.

Assets and the timeline

The Timeline panel under Assets is what the build packages. Its Picture track holds the clips in play order, each a video cut to an In and an Out point, drawn as wide as the frames it keeps and named after its file. Sound and Subtitle sit under it. Every video you import goes on the end as a clip from its first frame to its last, playing its own sound. The first WAV you import covers the whole timeline on Sound and the first subtitle file covers it on Subtitle, unless a clip already has its own. Drag a video from Assets onto the Picture track to put a clip after the one you drop it on. Drop a WAV or a subtitle file on the span under a clip to give that clip its own, or on the Sound or Subtitle label to cover the whole timeline, which replaces every clip's own. A channel set only covers the whole timeline. The ✕ on a span takes it off, and a clip then plays its own sound again. Removing an asset takes it off the timeline, and an empty timeline takes the next video still in Assets.

Click a clip to select it. In and Out are timecodes in the clip's source, HH:MM:SS:FF, and the Out frame is the first one left out. While the preview plays the clip's video, Set in from playhead and Set out from playhead take the frame on screen. ← and → move the clip one place and Remove takes it off. − and + zoom the tracks. Clicking the ruler or a clip moves the preview to that frame while it plays a clip of the timeline, and loads the other clip's video first when the frame is in another clip.

A timeline of one clip builds the way a single video always has: In and Out trim it the way Trim off the head and Trim off the tail do, and Reel length, Split at and Split at source chapter marks under Properties, Reels cut it into reels. The reel starts Split at lists show as markers on the ruler, and Split reel here adds one at the playhead and writes it into Split at. With more than one clip the trims, Still length and the reel fields are greyed out: the reels start at the markers on the ruler, Split reel here adds one at the playhead and clicking a marker removes it. Every clip has to run at the first clip's frame rate, a clip of another size is fitted onto the container the first clip goes into, and the clips' sound is joined in order. Burn subtitles into the picture, Right-eye video, DCI HDR, generic tone mapping and the picture fades are refused on more than one clip.

A project saved before the timeline opens with Reel 1's video as the first clip, playing Reel 1's WAV, and every later reel's video after it in reel order. Those later reels were never built before, so the status line names each one that moved.

The first video you import is probed, and a line under Properties, Picture says what it found, such as From the source: 1920x1080, 25 fps, bt709, 179 Mbit/s. Frame Rate takes the source's rate when it is a whole rate the list offers, and 24, 30, 48 or 60 for a 23.976, 29.97, 47.952 or 59.94 fps source. Frame Rate's Auto (from source) packages at that same rate. Source colour space takes Rec. 709, Rec. 2020, DCI-P3 or X'Y'Z' from the source's colour tags, and stays at Rec. 709 when the source declares none. Resolution's Auto option names the container the uncropped source goes into. A field the probe set has a dashed bar on its left, and editing it removes the bar. The probe never changes a field you edited or a delivery profile set. Bandwidth is what the encode aims for, so the source's bit rate is only shown.

Mono WAVs that share a name up to a channel suffix become one channel set on Sound when you drop them together or pick them with + Audio. The suffix is the last part of the name after _, ., - or a space, and names a lane: L, R, C, LFE, Ls, Rs, Lc, Rc, BsL, BsR, HI or VI. So mix_L.wav, mix.R.wav and mix C.wav are one set, and the build puts each file on its lane. A WAV that does not fit a set, because it has no suffix, more than one channel or a lane the set already has, stays its own asset. Under Audio, Channel mapping names the file behind each row of a set, and the dotted cells show where each file or channel goes. A dotted cell routes at 0 dB until you type in its row, and then that row routes only the cells you typed.

Preview plays the asset or package you selected, or the first clip's video when nothing is selected.

Gain and fades

Under Audio, Gain raises or lowers the whole sound track by that many dB. With a gain typed, the loudness target is a reference for Set gain and is not applied automatically. With Gain empty, a loudness target such as leqm=85 or lufs=-20 sets the level, held under the true-peak ceiling. Fade in and Fade out bring the sound up from silence at the start and down to silence at the end over that many seconds. Under Trim & Stills, Picture fade in and Picture fade out do the same to the picture, from and to black. Fades and the level run on what is left after a trim, so the fades land on the first and last frames the package carries. A still hold takes no picture fade, and the build refuses one.

Measure, beside Loudness target, runs the sound steps a build runs and shows two lines. Source is the sound after routing, the channel map, the upmix, the delay and the trim. Delivered is the track the package will carry, after the loudness target or the gain and the fades. A third line lists what was applied, or Source track as is. With a loudness target typed, Set gain to reach target puts the difference between the target and the Source measurement of the same kind in Gain, to a tenth of a dB, which then sets the level in place of the target. Measure again to confirm the Delivered level.

Under the lines, a graph draws the Delivered track's short-term loudness, the loudness of the last 3 s read every 100 ms, from -60 to 0 LUFS against time in minutes and seconds. The solid line across it is the integrated loudness. The dashed vertical line marks where the true peak falls, labelled with its dBTP value. With a target in LUFS a dashed line shows it. A Leq(m) target has no line, because Leq(m) is not on the LUFS scale. A stretch of digital silence leaves a gap in the curve, and a track under 3 s draws no curve.

Picture size and position

Under Source Picture, Scale sizes the picture against the container with its aspect kept: 100 fits the container, under 100 leaves wider black bands, and over 100 fills past it and cuts the edges off at the frame. Offset X and Y move the picture that many container pixels right and down, and negative values move it left and up. A picture smaller than the frame stops at the frame's edge, and a larger one stops where its own edge reaches the frame. The line under Auto-crop shows the size and position the build will use. From a terminal, create --picture-scale 90 --picture-offset 0,-20 does the same.

Preview in container, at the end of Source Picture, plays the preview through the build's own picture filters, so it shows the frame the build writes: the crop, rotation, flip, scale and offset, on the container with its black bands. The safe area, aspect mask, centre cross and thirds are then measured against that frame, and the crop overlay is off since the frame has the crop taken off already. The view follows the fields as you edit them. It is off by default and not saved in the project. A JPEG 2000 source plays through the grok player, which has no picture filters, so the box is greyed out for one.

Saved presets

A preset keeps the Properties settings you reuse across films: picture, encode, packaging, audio with its channel map, the subtitle and burn-in look, and the naming metadata. It leaves out what belongs to one film: the title, the output folder, the key file, the right eye, Atmos, the closed caption and sign-language files, the burn-in subtitle file, versions, Split at, the trim, the still length and the version number, content version and version flags. Set Properties up, press Save as preset… beside Delivery profile and give it a name. Saving under a name you already have asks before it replaces that preset.

Saved presets are listed under Saved in the Delivery profile list, after the built-in profiles. Picking one sets every field it holds and marks them the way a profile does, with the hint Set by Festival: 78 settings. Edit any field to override. A field you then edit loses its mark and keeps your value. A choice the list no longer offers is named in the status line. Delete preset removes the one picked. Export… writes every preset to one file, and Import… on another machine adds the presets in that file, replacing any with the same name and naming them in the status line. Presets live in presets.json beside the settings file.

From a terminal

dcpwizard create --title "My Film" --video movie.mov --audio mix.wav --output ./dcp
dcpwizard create --title "My Film" --video movie.mov --output ./dcp --preset Festival

--preset reads the same presets.json. A flag you give wins over the preset's value, and so does a flag that rules the preset's out, such as --container-dims over the preset's resolution. A preset field create has no flag for is named in a warning and left out. --preset and --profile are refused together.

--timeline programme.json takes the place of --video: every clip in the timeline file, each cut to its in and out point, is built into one package with a reel at each reel start. dcpwizard conform writes such a file as timeline.json, and the README describes its fields.

Encrypt it

In the app

Open Settings. Under Certificates, press Create signing chain…, type the organization, and pick a folder. The app fills in the signing certificate, its key, the intermediate, and the root, and saves them.

Back on Project, under Properties, Options, tick Encrypt and choose a Key Output File that is not inside the package. Press Build DCP. Keep that keys file. A package that was already built in the clear stays clear.

From a terminal

dcpwizard certificate chain --organization "Studio" --output ~/dcp-certs
dcpwizard create --title "My Film" --video movie.mov --output ./dcp \
  --encrypt --key-out ~/keys/my-film.json \
  --signer-cert ~/dcp-certs/signer.pem \
  --signer-key ~/dcp-certs/signer.key \
  --signer-chain ~/dcp-certs/intermediate.pem \
  --signer-chain ~/dcp-certs/root.pem

Make a KDM

In the app

Open Encryption & KDM. Under Make a KDM, pick the encrypted DCP. CPL ID and Content Title fill in. Pick the screen's Recipient Certificate, the Keys File from the build, Valid From, Valid To, and an output folder. Press Make KDM. The signature is the chain in Settings.

Tick DKDM for this machine when the KDM is for you. The recipient is then the certificate in Settings, so you can reissue it later.

From a terminal

dcpwizard kdm \
  --cpl-id <cpl-uuid> \
  --content-title "My Film" \
  --cert screen.pem \
  --signer-cert ~/dcp-certs/signer.pem \
  --signer-key ~/dcp-certs/signer.key \
  --signer-chain ~/dcp-certs/intermediate.pem \
  --signer-chain ~/dcp-certs/root.pem \
  --keys ~/keys/my-film.json \
  --valid-from now --valid-to "2 weeks" \
  -o screen.kdm.xml

Re-wrap a DKDM

In the app

On the same screen, under From a DKDM, pick the DKDM and the new screen certificate. If the DKDM was made for this machine, tick Unwrap with this machine's key. Leave Valid From and Valid To empty to keep the DKDM's window. Press Make KDM.

From a terminal

dcpwizard kdm-rewrap \
  --dkdm my.dkdm.xml \
  --dkdm-key ~/dcp-certs/recipient.key \
  --cert screen.pem \
  --signer-cert ~/dcp-certs/signer.pem \
  --signer-key ~/dcp-certs/signer.key \
  --signer-chain ~/dcp-certs/intermediate.pem \
  --signer-chain ~/dcp-certs/root.pem \
  -o screen.kdm.xml

Export the recipient certificate

In the app

In Settings, Certificates, press Create recipient certificate… and pick a folder. That needs the signing chain from earlier. If DCP-o-matic is already on this machine, press Import from DCP-o-matic… instead. That copies its decryption certificate and private key.

Press Export certificate… and send that file to the QC software. It is the public certificate. The private key stays here, and the button will not write a file that also holds a key.

The QC tool uses it to aim a KDM at this copy of DCP Wizard. To open one of your encrypted DCPs over there, give it a KDM aimed at the QC tool, or the keys file from the build.

From a terminal

dcpwizard certificate generate -t leaf \
  --cn "Studio" --organization "Studio" \
  --issuer-cert ~/dcp-certs/intermediate.pem \
  --issuer-key ~/dcp-certs/intermediate.key \
  --output-cert ~/dcp-certs/recipient.pem \
  --output-key ~/dcp-certs/recipient.key
dcpwizard preferences import-dcpomatic

Build several DCPs

In the app

Build DCP adds the build to Jobs. Builds run one at a time, in the order you pressed Build DCP. While the first one runs, change the Title, or open the next project from the Project menu, or choose New and add its assets, and press Build DCP again. It shows in Jobs as queued and starts when the one before it finishes. Build DCP is greyed while the Title matches a build that is queued or running, since two builds with one title would write the same folder. After a build finishes, the button reads DCP Built and stays greyed until you change the project or a setting the build reads, or delete the built folder. Reopening the project keeps it that way.

Open Jobs from the sidebar to see each build's state and progress. Press ✕ on a queued or running row to cancel it. A queued row has Run next, Earlier and Later buttons, which reorder the queue. The running build never moves.

From a terminal

# Build a package from every master that lands in ./incoming
dcpwizard watch ./incoming --output ./packages -- --standard smpte

# Run the daemon in one terminal and queue from another, batch add exits 1 when the daemon is not running
dcpwizard daemon
dcpwizard batch add -T create-dcp -p '{"title":"My Film","standard":"Smpte","resolution":"TwoK","content_type":"Feature","frame_rate_num":24,"frame_rate_den":1,"max_bitrate_mbps":250,"encrypt":false,"stereo_3d":false,"container_width":0,"container_height":0,"output_dir":"./dcp","j2k_dir":"./j2k","audio_path":"./audio.wav","audio_input_order":"Canonical51","subtitle_language":"en","reel_length_minutes":0}'
dcpwizard batch list
dcpwizard batch move 3 --before 2

The daemon's jobs show in the same Jobs panel, with their own Run next, Earlier and Later buttons.

Validate a DCP

In the app

Open Verify and press Validate. The view starts on the package the preview last played, else the DCP opened with Open DCP, else the last build, else the package folder beside the open project file. Select DCP… picks another, which then stays. The app runs the strict SMPTE check and then the Bv2.1 profile check, and a package with only warnings passes. Inspect MXF essence is off by default. Ticked, it reads every picture frame's codestream and names the largest frame against the DCI cap, which takes about as long as reading the picture file once. Inspect, in the bottom bar after a build, opens this view on the new package and runs it.

While Validate runs, a bar under the boxes reads Checking hashes or Scanning frames with the percent done, and Scanning frames starts from 0% for each reel. Cancel beside Validate stops the run, and the results then read Verification cancelled.

After a run, Save report… writes the findings as an HTML page, and on Linux Save PDF… prints the same page to a PDF. The save dialog offers <package folder>-verify.html or .pdf beside the package. The report holds the Bv2.1 findings below the strict check's, and its verdict passes only when both pass.

From a terminal

dcpwizard verify "./dcp/My Film" --strict
dcpwizard verify "./dcp/My Film" --strict --no-picture-check -o verify.html
dcpwizard verify "./dcp/My Film" --strict --progress

--progress prints progress hashes <done> <total> and progress frames <done> <total> lines to stderr, one each time the whole percent changes. Hashes count bytes and frames count each reel's picture frames.

See the reels on the Timeline

In the app

Press Open DCP on Project and pick a package, then open Reels & Timeline. The Picture and Sound tracks show one block per reel of the package's first CPL, with the reel's length. Click the ruler or a track to move the preview to that point. With no package open it shows the project's timeline, one block per reel from its reel starts, and follows the preview while it plays a clip.

From a terminal

dcpwizard info "./dcp/Two Reels"

Play a package

In the app

Select an asset, or press Open DCP and pick a package, then press ▶ Preview. A DCP, a CPL, a picture MXF or a folder of codestreams plays through grok, and anything else through libmpv. An encrypted package opens the Content keys dialog. Pick the KDM and the Recipient Private Key it was issued to, or the KEYS.json from the build, and press Play. The keys are reused for that package until the app closes.

The QC strip under the picture has Safe, Aspect, Cross and Thirds overlays, Crop for the crop the build applies, Sub and CC to show the subtitle and closed caption tracks, and Decode to play at full, half or quarter size. ⛶ in the preview header, or F, fills the screen. The controls and the cursor hide three seconds after the pointer last moved and come back on a pointer move or a key press. Press ⛶ again or Escape to return to the window.

Open Player under the preview and tick Level meter to see one bar per channel, named by its lane, with the peak held for a second and a clip mark at 0 dBFS. On a DCP the bars follow the sound as it plays. On any other file they run 0.3 to 0.6 s ahead of what you hear.

Queue, beside Play after a build, and + beside a project in the Project menu's recent list, add the package to the playlist under the transport bar. Click a row to play it, and the next row starts when it ends. ↑ and ↓ reorder the rows and ✕ removes one. The playlist lasts until the app closes.

Encode to JPEG 2000

In the app

Open Tools. Under Encode to JPEG 2000, pick the image sequence folder as Input, pick an Output Directory, set Bandwidth (Mbps) and press Encode. The codestreams go in a j2k folder inside the output. Resolution fits each frame into the 2K or 4K full container, and Frame Rate is the rate the bitrate is budgeted at.

From a terminal

dcpwizard encode -i ./frames -o ./encoded --bandwidth 230

Transcode a video to frames

In the app

Under Transcode Video, pick the Input Video and an Output Directory, choose Format and Bit Depth, and press Transcode. The frames come out as frame_00000001.tiff and on, at the source's size, ready to be the Input of Encode to JPEG 2000. A 16-bit TIFF of a 2K frame is about 12 MB, so a 90 minute feature fills well over a terabyte.

From a terminal

dcpwizard transcode -i movie.mov -o ./frames --format tiff --bit-depth 16

Measure loudness

In the app

To measure the sound a build will package, use Measure under Audio in Properties, see Gain and fades. Under Loudness Measurement, pick the Audio File and press Measure. It reports integrated loudness in LUFS, true peak in dBTP and the loudness range. The file has to be a WAV or a sound MXF out of a DCP. The picker also offers FLAC, AIFF and MP3, and those fail.

From a terminal

dcpwizard loudness mix.wav

Copy a DCP to a drive

In the app

Under Copy DCP, pick the Source DCP folder and the Destination, then press Copy DCP. The package lands in a folder of the same name inside the destination. Each file is read back after the copy and its hash checked against the source.

From a terminal

dcpwizard copy --src "./dcp/My Film" --dst ./drive

Make a QC report

In the app

Under QC Report, pick the DCP Directory and press Generate Report. The report is an HTML page written beside the package as its folder name plus .qc.html, never inside it, so the package stays clean for delivery.

From a terminal

dcpwizard report --dcp "./dcp/My Film" -o report.html

Convert SRT to SMPTE subtitles

In the app

A build converts an SRT on the Subtitle track by itself, so this is only for a standalone SMPTE subtitle XML. Under SRT → SMPTE Subtitle Conversion, pick the SRT and an Output Folder and press Convert. The XML takes the SRT's name, and with no folder picked it goes beside the SRT.

From a terminal

dcpwizard subtitle-convert -i subs.srt -o subs.xml -l en --fps 24 --vposition 8

Extract subtitles

In the app

Under Subtitle Extraction, press Folder for a DCP or File for a subtitle XML or MXF, choose Format and press Extract. The text goes to subtitles.srt or subtitles.txt in Output Folder, or in the folder that holds the input when Output Folder is empty. Cue times come out rounded to the DCP's frames.

From a terminal

dcpwizard subtitle-extract -i "./dcp/Subbed" -o subtitles.srt

Burn in subtitles

In the app

Subtitle Burn-in makes a review copy, a video file rather than a package. For a DCP with burnt-in text, set Burn subtitles into the picture in Properties and build. Burn In writes the video's name plus _burnin.mp4 into Output Directory, or beside the video. It takes SRT, ASS/SSA, WebVTT, and SMPTE or Interop XML or MXF, which it converts first.

From a terminal

dcpwizard burnin -i movie.mov -s subs.srt -o review.mp4

Convert to a DCI container

In the app

Target Conversion scales, crops or letterboxes a video to one of the six DCI containers. Convert writes the video's name plus the container, for example clip_2k-scope.mov, into Output Directory, or beside the video. From a terminal the codec follows the output's name, so scope.mov comes out as H.264.

From a terminal

dcpwizard convert -i movie.mov -t 2k-scope -m letterbox -o scope.mov

Re-ingest a package

In the app

Under Re-ingest Package, pick the Package Directory and press Rebuild ASSETMAP & PKL. It writes a new ASSETMAP and PKL listing every asset file in the folder, deletes the old ones, and leaves the essence alone. With a signing chain in Settings the new PKL is signed, which an encrypted package needs to pass Validate.

From a terminal

dcpwizard ingest-package ./pkg
dcpwizard ingest-package ./pkg \
  --signer-cert ~/dcp-certs/signer.pem \
  --signer-key ~/dcp-certs/signer.key \
  --signer-chain ~/dcp-certs/intermediate.pem \
  --signer-chain ~/dcp-certs/root.pem

Export a DCP

In the app

Under Export DCP, press DCP folder or CPL or MXF, choose Format, press Save as for the Output and press Export. A composition exports with all its reels in order as one file. A picture MXF on its own exports silent unless a Sound override is given. An encrypted DCP needs its KDM with the Recipient private key it was issued to, or the KEYS.json from the build.

From a terminal

dcpwizard export --input "./dcp/My Film" -o my-film.mp4 --format h264
dcpwizard export --input ./dcp/Locked --keys ~/keys/locked.json -o locked.mp4

Make a Version File

In the app

Open Version File (Supplemental DCP) and pick the Original Version (OV) Directory and an Output Folder. Under Replacement Reels, set each Reel number and pick its Picture, a folder of J2K frames or an MXF through MXF…, or its Sound, a WAV, which a 16-bit file is widened to 24 bits for, press + Reel for the next one, and press Create Version File. The VF goes in a new folder named after Title, which defaults to the OV's title with _VF on the end. A replacement has to run exactly as long as the reel it replaces, or Create Version File refuses it. Validate the VF against its OV from a terminal, as below.

From a terminal

dcpwizard create-vf --ov "./dcp/Two Reels" --output ./vf \
  --replace-picture 2=./j2k-reel2 --replace-sound 2=reel2.wav
dcpwizard verify "./vf/Two Reels_VF" --strict --ov "./dcp/Two Reels"

Rebuild a DCP

In the app

Open Rebuild DCP and pick the Source DCP folder. Its title, standard, picture size and active area, frame rate, reels and kind are listed under it, and its title and ratings fill Title and Ratings. A DCP with more than one CPL, an encrypted one, or one with closed captions or an Atmos track is refused there with the reason. Change Title, edit, add or remove Ratings, pick an Output Folder and press Rebuild. Container fits the picture into another container with black bars, Custom WxH takes any size up to 4K, and Bit rate sets the picture's Mbit/s and the library items'. Only a new container or a bit rate re-encodes the picture, otherwise its track is copied byte for byte. Pick a library item and press Add to head or Add to tail to play it before or after the DCP, and drag the chips to reorder them. The new OV goes in a new folder named after Title, and the source is never changed. Cancel stops the rebuild and removes what it wrote.

From a terminal

dcpwizard assemble --input "./dcp/My Film" --output ./rebuilt --title "My Film UK" \
  --rating "http://www.bbfc.co.uk=12A" --head-item "Studio Ident"
dcpwizard transcode-dcp --input "./dcp/My Film" --output ./scope --container 2k-scope
dcpwizard assemble --input ./scope --output ./rebuilt --title "My Film Scope"

Use the keyboard

In the app

Hold Alt to see the underlined letter on a button, and press Alt with that letter to press it. Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 open the sidebar views from the top, so Version File is Ctrl+6, Rebuild DCP Ctrl+7, Jobs Ctrl+8 and Settings Ctrl+9. Ctrl+Enter runs the action of the section the focus is in. In a path field Alt+Down opens its file dialog, and Escape leaves a text field. Alt+P opens the Project menu, where N, O, S and A run New, Open, Save and Save As and the arrow keys pick a recent project. After a build, Ctrl+Shift+P plays it, Ctrl+Shift+A queues it, Ctrl+Shift+E opens it in Verify and Ctrl+Shift+F shows its folder.

Ctrl+K lists every shortcut. Click one and press the new keys. Backspace clears it and Escape cancels. ↺ puts one shortcut back to its default and Reset all puts back every one. Your changes are saved.

Change the settings

In the app

Open Settings, change what you need and press Save. Terminal commands read the same file, so Encode on the GPU, Encode threads, Grok License and Report black and frozen runs while encoding apply there too, and their flags override them. A build goes to Default Output Directory when Folder is empty, and ISDCF Facility Code fills the mastering facility in the CPL and the ISDCF name when the project leaves it blank. If grok cannot start the GPU plugin, Save unticks Encode on the GPU and the status bar says why. Standard, Resolution, Frame Rate and Bandwidth are what a new project starts with. Reset puts every setting back to its default.

From a terminal

dcpwizard preferences show
dcpwizard preferences set encode-threads 8
